## Belt line
While drawing the idler support I noticed that the belt can't line up with the clamp under the bed. The two pulleys are at the same height, so the lower run of the belt is straight, but the clamp is higher than the top of the pulleys. The top run goes up to the clamp and down again, making a triangle, and the triangle changes shape as the bed moves towards one end. I asked whether that only changes the force of the motor or really affects something. The answer, in short:
- **It affects the accuracy.** GRBL assumes that each mm of belt the motor winds is 1 mm of bed travel. With the belt at an angle, the belt runs along the hypotenuse of the triangle, not along the rods. The error grows near the motor, where the angle is steepest. With 5 mm of height difference, the belt is about 0.6 mm longer with the clamp 20 mm from the motor, 0.25 mm at 50 mm and 0.06 mm at 200 mm. The error changes with the position, so calibrating the steps/mm can't remove it.
- **The tension changes along the travel.** The two sides of the triangle add up to more near the ends than in the middle. The belt is a closed loop, so it gets tighter at the ends and slacker in the middle.
- **Part of the pull goes up or down**, and that also changes with the position. It loads the rods and can tilt the bed a little, which changes the pressure of the pen.
- **Wear is small**, because the angle is in the plane of the pulley. What wears a belt is a sideways misalignment that makes it climb a flange.

What has to be straight is the **belt line**: the top of the motor pulley, the top of the idler and the face of the clamp where the belt's teeth sit. When both pulleys have the same diameter, both runs are then parallel to the rods. The aim is zero difference. Up to 1 mm is acceptable, about 0.025 mm of error at 20 mm from the motor, and shims under the clamp give a fine adjustment after assembly.

In my case the two pulleys already line up with each other, so it's the clamp that has to come down to the belt line. If a lower clamp hits the motor or the pulley, both pulleys can go up by the same amount instead.

In the model, the motor pulley and the idler both have 20 teeth and their axes are 30 mm above the base, so the top run of the belt is at about 36.4 mm and the underside of the bed at 51.5 mm. The clamp has to reach about 15 mm under the bed.

## Machining the base on the CNC
The base (500 × 500 × 15 mm, R20 corners, 28 holes with counterbores) was machined on the lab's CNC router, out of a 600 × 600 × 15 mm MDF sheet. The setup was done in the Manufacture workspace of Fusion 360 and was the slow part: registering the tools and setting up each operation.

| Operation | Tool | What it cuts |
|---|---|---|
| Drill1 | T2, Ø3.5 mm drill, 10,000 rpm, 1,000 mm/min | The 28 through holes |
| 2D Contour2 | T1, Ø4 mm flat end mill, 2 flutes, 10,000 rpm, 3,100 mm/min, plunging at 800 mm/min | The 28 counterbores, Ø6 × 3.3 mm |
| 2D Contour1 | T1, the same end mill | The outline, in steps of 1.6 mm, 0.1 mm below the sheet |

- **Stock:** a fixed 600 × 600 × 15 mm box, with the base in the middle and the origin at a corner of the top face. The counterbores are on the underside of the base, so the sheet was machined with that face up.
- **Post processor:** Mach3 (`mach3mill.cps`).
- **NC programs:** 5 are left in the file from the attempts: `furação`, `usinagem`, `USINAGEM2`, `CONTORNO` and `CONTORN3`.

## Problems → fixes
- **The CNC's Y axis was faulty.** The through holes and the outline came out in the right place. The counterbores came out the right size but in the wrong place, shifted up or to the side of their hole, on 6 or 7 holes. Each run put them somewhere else. We checked the file and ran it several times, and the lab technicians confirmed that the fault was in the machine. → I drilled the counterbores by hand with a hand drill, around the through holes. With no depth stop, some came out deeper than others, but all of them hide the screw heads under the base. The misplaced recesses stay as they are for now. Later I'll fill them with wood glue and MDF dust, or resin, only for looks.
- **The screws of the motor mount.** The 2 screws that hold the Y motor mount to the base go up from under the base into the foot, under the motor. In the model I put the nut pockets too high in the foot, so each screw had to end exactly in its nut without touching the motor. → I adjusted each screw with washers and nuts until it stood at the right height, then fitted the mount over it, turned it and tightened. It worked. The mistake was putting the motor over its own screws: they should be outside the motor, on the sides of the foot. This was a check on [2026-10-20](2026-10-20.md#checks-before-building).

## Checks before building
- **The clamp at the belt line**, about 15 mm under the bed, so the top run is straight from the motor pulley to the idler.
- **Sideways line-up of the Y belt.** In the model, the toothed part of the motor pulley (7 mm wide) is centred 1.2 mm to the side of the idler's (6.5 mm wide), so a 6 mm belt can't run straight on both without touching a flange. When fitting the belt, slide the pulley about 1.2 mm towards the end of the motor shaft (it is held by grub screws).
- **Height of the Y motor.** The printed mount has the 9 mm foot, so in the model the top of the motor is 0.5 mm under the bed. Check it on the machine when the bed goes on.
- **Foot screws of the X motor mount.** The new mount repeats the layout that caused trouble today: its 2 foot holes are under the motor, and the motor sits right on the 5 mm foot, so there's no room for a nut or a screw head on top. Move them to ears at the sides before printing it.
- **Nut of the idler axle.** The idler supports were printed without a hex pocket for the M5 nut, so it needs a second key to tighten.
- **Joint of the idler support.** The fillet between each tab and its foot is R2, and 3 to 5 mm was suggested on the 22nd. Keep an eye on it once the belt is tight.
