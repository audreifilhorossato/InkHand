## The fan
A 40 mm fan from the lab (Botto Brasil, sleeve bearing, 12 V, 0.04 A), to blow on the two drivers (F017). I asked whether it was strong enough, at what height to put it, whether it was needed at all, how to power it and which side is which. The answers, in short:
- **Size.** A 40 mm fan is enough. The big hub is normal for this size.
- **Height.** 10 to 15 mm between the blades and the top of the heatsinks is ideal. Too close leaves a dead spot under the hub; too far and the air spreads before it reaches the drivers. I only have room for about 5 mm, which works: centred between the two drivers, the hub is over the gap between them and both heatsinks get air from the blades (F018).
- **Is it needed?** At plotter currents (the DRV8825s are set to 1.0 A, Vref 0.50 V) the drivers get warm and work without a fan. It's worth it inside a closed box or with more current. It's cheap and quiet, so I'm fitting it, as insurance against a driver losing steps when it heats up in a long drawing. Test: run a 20-minute drawing without the fan and touch the heatsink; if you can keep your finger there, it's fine.
- **Power.** It's a 12 V fan, so it goes on the 12 V input, before the LM2596, never on the 5 V output. Red to +, black to −; reversed, it doesn't turn.
- **Current.** Everything on the 12 V supply is in parallel: the supply holds 12 V and each load draws only what it needs. The currents add up: the 2 motors up to about 1.5 to 2 A, the Arduino and the servo through the LM2596 about 0.3 to 0.5 A, the fan 0.04 A. About 2.5 A in total, well under the 5 A of the supply.
- **Which side.** The air comes out on the side with the sticker. To blow on the drivers, **the sticker faces down, towards the board**. To check, switch it on and hold a hand or a strip of paper on each side. Many fans also have two small arrows on the frame, one for the airflow and one for the rotation.
